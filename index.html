The Floating Silent Threat

Marine pollution isn't just floating visible trash; it is a complex ecological crisis infiltrating deep sea trenches, food webs, and coastal habitats worldwide.

01 / Ghost Nets & Entanglement

Derelict Gear: The Unseen Predators

Abandoned commercial fishing gear—known as "ghost nets"—accounts for nearly 10% of all marine debris. Left floating indefinitely, these nets continually capture sea turtles, dolphins, whales, and seals in a deadly cycle of non-stop entrapment.

  • Takes up to 600 years to decompose in seawater
  • Destroys coral reefs by smothering fragile structures
"Over 640,000 tons of ghost gear are left in our oceans every single year."
UN Environmental Programme
02 / Microplastics & Chemical Toxins

The Invisible Poison in the Marine Food Web

Under sunlight and ocean currents, larger plastics break down into microscopic fragments under 5mm in size. Marine creatures mistake these particles for food, allowing toxic pollutants to bioaccumulate all the way up to human dinner plates.

  • Found in 100% of sea turtle species surveyed
  • Absorbs hazardous organic pollutants from seawater
"By 2050, plastic in the ocean is projected to outweigh all fish by weight."
World Economic Forum

The Marine Plastic Journey

Click through the stages below to see how land waste ripples outward into deep ocean ecosystems.

Stage 1

Urban Runoff & Improper Disposal

Single-use plastics leave city streets and storm drains, washed away by rainfall into rivers.

1

Over 80% of ocean plastic originates from land-based sources due to inadequate waste management infrastructure.

Stage 2

Riverine Transport to Coasts

1,000 major rivers worldwide act as high-speed conveyor belts carrying plastics directly into oceans.

2

Heavy monsoon seasons accelerate this river-to-sea debris transport by over 300%.

Stage 3

Gyre Accumulation & Fragmentation

Ocean currents trap waste into massive floating garbage patches, breaking down into microplastics.

3

The Great Pacific Garbage Patch now spans an area three times the size of France.
